## THE ONE SENTENCE VERSION

Email alone is nearly worthless, camps alone are decent, and **an email sent ten days
before he attends that coach's camp is the actual mechanism**. The email tells a coach to
watch for him. The camp lets them. Neither works without the other.

---

## WHY CAMPS BEAT EVERYTHING ELSE FOR HIM SPECIFICALLY

Camps and ID clinics are described in the recruiting literature as the highest-leverage
thing a high school recruit can do: highlight reels open the door, camps are where you walk
through it. Three reasons that applies double to him:

1. **You buy your way in.** No ranking, no invitation, no selection committee. For an
   unranked kid this is the only setting where he gets the same live look a top-50 recruit
   gets.
2. **Juniors specifically benefit most from multi-school camps** where 10+ programs evaluate
   at one event. He is a junior. That is the exact recommendation for his class year.
3. **His differentiator is visual and instant.** 6'5" and 226 at close defense is five inches
   and thirty-five pounds past the positional average. A coach clocks that from across a
   field in two seconds. It does not require a highlight reel to land, and it does not
   survive being described in an email that gets deleted in five seconds.

Coaches at competitive programs receive **100 to 300 recruiting emails a day**. That is why
email alone fails. It is also why email works fine as a pointer to a camp he is already
registered for.

## THE ORDER OF OPERATIONS

Ranked by leverage. Do them in this order, not in parallel, except where noted.

### 1. Film. Within 14 days. Everything is blocked behind this.

Three to five minutes of **game film**, not a highlight reel of big hits.

For defense specifically coaches look for communication, successful stick checks, and smart
quick decisions with the ball after a takeaway. Include full possessions and defensive
recovery, not just clips where he wins. Coaches consistently prioritise players who make
smart decisions and compete hard over players with occasional highlight plays.

Two clips at the front: one where he shuts down a dodge from X, one where he causes a
turnover and makes a clean outlet. Then let it run.

Host it somewhere with a stable link. Hudl, Vimeo, YouTube unlisted. The link must work on a
phone without a login.

**Without this, none of the rest of the plan functions.** It is the fourteen-day blocker.

### 2. Decide the summer 2027 club. This fall. Highest-stakes single decision.

This is the one people get wrong because the deadline feels far away and is not.

The summer circuit is where the volume of evaluation happens. The NLF's one-day 2028
showcase puts players in front of **70+ Division I coaches** on one field at Loyola. The
Adrenaline Challenge has drawn 200+ teams in front of **75+ NCAA men's coaches**. NLF
National Championships run 11-12 July 2027 at Stony Brook for 2028-2030 teams.

Those events are reached **through his club team**. If his current club is not on the
NLF or Adrenaline circuit, changing clubs is the single biggest lever available to him and
it has to happen this fall, because rosters for summer 2027 form over the winter.

**Question to answer this month:** which national events is his current club actually
attending in summer 2027? If the answer is none or regional only, start the conversation
about moving now.

### 3. Winter multi-school ID camps. December through February. The dense window.

This is where a junior gets the most evaluation per dollar. Target **three to four** of
these, prioritising events that list 10+ college programs in attendance.

These fit the training calendar perfectly. The winter block (8 Dec to 27 Feb) is a strength
and conversion phase with lower speed volume, so a Saturday camp costs almost nothing in
training terms. Do not schedule one the day after a Monday max-velocity session.

### 4. Single-school prospect days at the five to eight schools he actually wants.

This is how you get in front of a specific staff rather than a crowd.

**Ivy programs matter most here.** Since academics are not a constraint, the Ivy tier is his
best realistic band, and Ivy coaches use their own camps as a primary evaluation tool.

Known specifics to check now, because they have deadlines:
- **Penn's Quaker Lacrosse Camp** is at Penn Park, open to 9th-12th grade, and has carried
  an application deadline of **1 February**. Verify this year's date directly.
- **Princeton's men's lacrosse prospect clinic** has historically run a one-day January
  format at Princeton Stadium.

Each school runs its own, dates move year to year, and they are published on the athletics
site. Check every school on the target list in October and put the deadlines on a calendar.

### 5. Email, timed to the camps. Not as a standalone campaign.

Build a list of **30 to 50 programs across tiers**. A smaller list misses opportunities, a
large generic blast lowers reply rates.

**Send each email ten to fourteen days before he attends that program's camp**, or before a
tournament where they will be present. That is the version that gets read, because the coach
can act on it.

---

## THE EMAIL

Coaches search their inbox later by grad year and position, so both go in the subject line.
Keep it 50 to 75 characters because coaches read on phones. Skip hype words.

**Subject:** `2028 Close D — 6'5" 226 — Film + Winter Schedule`

Putting the measurables in the subject line is not standard advice. It is right for him
specifically, because his size is the thing that makes a coach open the message, and it is
verifiable rather than a claim.

**Body, and he writes it, not you.** Coaches read for whether the player wrote it.

> Coach [Name],
>
> I'm a 2028 close defenseman at [High School], 6'5" and 226 as of [date]. I play club for
> [Club].
>
> Film: [link]. It's three full possessions and two takeaways, not a highlight reel.
>
> [One specific, true sentence about their program. Their defensive scheme, a player whose
> game he studies, why the academic fit works. One sentence. Not flattery.]
>
> I'm registered for your prospect day on [date] and wanted you to know I'll be there.
>
> [One direct question they can answer in a line from the road, e.g. "What do you look for
> in a 2028 close defenseman in your slide package?"]
>
> [Name], [phone], [GPA/scores], [club coach name and contact]

The fastest way to get ignored is ending with "any feedback?" Ask something specific enough
to answer in one line.

## THE TIER LIST

Three schools is the wrong frame. There are roughly 70 to 80 D1 men's lacrosse programs.

| Tier | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Reach** | 5-8 | Penn, UNC, Duke and peers. Keep them. A live look is not wasted at 6'5", and the Ivy tier is genuinely live now that academics are clear. |
| **Target** | 15-20 | Where the offer probably comes from. Mid-table D1 where his body is immediately interesting. |
| **Likely** | 10-15 | Lower D1 and strong D3, where a 6'5" pole is a recruiting coup. |

The failure mode to avoid: aiming at three schools, getting nothing, and concluding D1 was
never realistic when forty programs would have taken a look and never knew he existed.

---

## WHAT I WOULD NOT SPEND MONEY ON YET

Paid recruiting profile services. I did not find evidence that coaches source recruits from
them, and the mechanism that demonstrably works (a coach physically watching him at their own
camp) costs the same money and produces a real evaluation. **This is my judgment, not a
sourced finding.** If someone shows you a program that produced offers, reassess.

---

## THE HONEST ODDS

Penn, UNC and Duke specifically: low. Not zero.

Duke's recent classes carry top-20 national recruits. Those staffs see every ranked pole in
the country. An unranked player gets into that room by being seen live or vouched for hard
by a coach they trust.

But rankings in lacrosse run **downstream** of coach interest, not upstream. The recognised
signal that an unranked player is about to get ranked is when he starts drawing interest
from programs that look like reaches. Coaches move first and the lists follow.

The absence of a ranking is not the problem. The absence of anyone watching is the problem,
and that is the only thing this document is about.
