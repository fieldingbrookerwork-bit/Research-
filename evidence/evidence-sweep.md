### Summary

Bright Data's free tier is real, recurring, and card-free — and unusually well suited to agent workflows. The official MCP repo (README updated 2026-08-12) states: "5,000 free requests per month... No credit card required. Renews monthly," with no rollover. Account-wide, the same 5,000-credit monthly pool spans the Web Unlocker API, SERP API, Web Scraper API, and Scraper Studio at 1 credit per request/record (~$7.50 value); proxies and the standalone Browser API are excluded. On top sit a one-time $2/7-day playground credit and a $5/30-day bonus for adding a payment method. The MCP server exposes 69 tools — Google/Bing/Yandex SERP search, scrape-as-markdown with unblocking/CAPTCHA bypass, 60+ structured scrapers including LinkedIn person/company/jobs/people-search, Google Maps reviews, Crunchbase, and 13 remote-browser tools — via a hosted URL (mcp.brightdata.com) or npx, with no default rate limit (self-set via RATE_LIMIT). At the cap, "requests stop. No surprise charges — unless you have deposited funds," and a spend cap is settable, so runaway-bill risk is near zero. Signup needs only an email plus verification; KYC (company verification, possible video call, 24-72h) applies to Residential proxies — tightened for new zones after July 7, 2026 — not to the free-tier APIs. Compliance-wise, Bright Data officially supports scraping Google SERP, public LinkedIn, and Google Maps reviews through dedicated tools, but restricts collection to public data: its browser blocks login/password entry by default, and login-gated scraping requires a KYC exception. Two conflicts to note: the MCP README claims "browser automation included" in the free tier while the account docs exclude Browser API from free credits; and an Aug 2026 StationX review reports personal-email signups (e.g., Gmail) are blocked from the Web Unlocker API and proxy networks until a payment method is added — directly relevant to a $0 founder. Direct verification of brightdata.com was impossible here (egress-blocked); primary evidence comes from official GitHub repos plus search extracts of official pages.

### Zero-budget implications

Concretely, a $0 founder gets ~5,000 billable operations/month (~165/day): e.g., 2,000 SERP prospecting queries + 2,500 LinkedIn/company/Maps structured records + 500 page scrapes, orchestrated by AI agents through one MCP endpoint with no card on file. That is enough to build ~100-record lead-research deliverables roughly 40-50 times a month — genuinely sellable volume for niche lead-gen or competitive-intel services. Hard edges: credits vanish monthly (no rollover, no stockpiling), the pool is per-account (multiple free accounts violates ToS), browser-automation tools may not actually draw from free credits (conflicting official statements — test before promising clients), and signing up with a personal Gmail (as this founder would) reportedly gates the Web Unlocker API until a payment method is added — adding a card is free but breaks the strict 'never enter a card' rule. When the cap hits mid-job, requests simply stop (no overage bills), so agents need graceful-degradation logic. Stay strictly on public data: no login-gated LinkedIn scraping, or the account risks compliance suspension — Bright Data rejects/suspends accounts at scale.

### Red flags

- Could not open brightdata.com/docs.brightdata.com directly (network egress blocked in this environment); official-page figures were verified via Bright Data's own GitHub repos (primary, current to 2026-08-12) and search-engine extracts — re-confirm on the live pricing page before committing.
- Conflicting official statements on browser automation: MCP README comparison table says free tier includes 'browser automation included', but the account free-tier docs exclude the Browser API from the 5,000 monthly free credits ($8/GB billing suggests it needs deposited funds) — must be tested empirically.
- Aug 2026 review reports personal-email (Gmail) signups are blocked from Web Unlocker API and proxy networks until a payment method is added — conflicts with 'no credit card' marketing; the founder's Gmail-based signup may hit this gate (medium confidence, single secondary source).
- Free credits are per account and shared across a team; creating multiple accounts to multiply the 5,000/month would breach terms and Bright Data's compliance team actively suspends accounts ('hundreds, perhaps even thousands' rejected annually).
- Public-data-only policy: browser blocks password entry by default and login-gated scraping requires a KYC exception — most high-value LinkedIn data (emails, full profiles behind login) is out of scope; Meta v. Bright Data (Jan 2024) blessed logged-off public scraping but state-law claims still apply if you circumvent login walls.
- KYC tightened recently: new Residential proxy zones after 2026-07-07 require company verification — the free tier avoids this today, but the trend is toward stricter gating, so free-tier terms could tighten too.
- No rollover and a hard stop at the cap mean client work can die mid-month; unverified whether the 5,000 MCP requests and 5,000 account credits are one pool or two (they appear to be the same pool — 1 request = 1 credit — but no source states it explicitly; low confidence).

### Summary

Twilio's 2026 trial is a 30-day sandbox, not an operating tier. The old $15 credit is gone; trials now grant product-specific free units (100 SMS, 100 WhatsApp, 3,000 emails, 75 voice minutes) that expire at 30 days or on exhaustion. Trial restrictions are severe: SMS carries a "Sent from your Twilio trial account" watermark, outbound SMS/calls reach only numbers the founder has personally code-verified, calls cap at 10 minutes, and inbound calls must come from verified caller IDs. The decisive blocker for US SMS: trial accounts cannot register A2P 10DLC at all (paid account required), and since Sep 1, 2023 all unregistered US-bound 10DLC traffic is hard-blocked (error 30034) — meaning a trial account cannot deliver a single SMS to a real US customer handset from a local number; the only free test target is Twilio's Virtual Phone. The toll-free path is equally closed at $0: verification itself is free and takes ~3–5 business days, but requires a paid account, and unverified toll-free traffic has been fully blocked since Jan 31, 2024, with EIN/business-registration numbers required for new verifications by early 2026. The realistic minimum for a legitimate US SMS operation is an upgraded account plus $4 brand + $15 campaign vetting one-time, then ~$2/mo campaign + $1.15/mo number + $0.0079–$0.0083/segment (sources conflict) + $0.003–$0.005/segment carrier surcharges. Voice is cheaper ($1.15/mo number, $0.0085/min inbound, $0.014/min outbound) but still not $0. SendGrid's famous 100/day free email tier died May 27, 2025 — now a 60-day trial, then $19.95/mo. Conclusion: no customer-facing SMS or voice service can run at literally $0/month on Twilio in Aug 2026; the trial supports only a 30-day proof-of-concept demo to the founder's own verified phones.

### Zero-budget implications

Concretely, at $0/month a founder CANNOT run any real SMS/voice service on Twilio in Aug 2026. US SMS to actual customers is impossible on a trial (no A2P registration allowed + unregistered traffic hard-blocked), and toll-free verification requires a paid account. What $0 buys: a 30-day dev sandbox — build and demo the full AI-agent pipeline against the founder's own verified phone and the Virtual Phone (75 voice minutes, watermarked, 10-min call cap). Email fallback via Twilio/SendGrid is also dead as a permanent free tier (60-day trial only). The cheapest legitimate go-live is roughly $19–$25 one-time (account upgrade funding + $4 sole-prop brand + $15 campaign vetting) plus ~$3.15/mo fixed ($2 campaign + $1.15 number) before per-message costs — small, but not $0, and sole-prop registration approval is not instant. A voice-only inbound service (no SMS) is the closest to free: ~$1.15/mo + $0.0085/min after upgrade. Also note: A2P campaign vetting requires documented opt-in consent — AI-driven cold SMS outreach would fail vetting and violate TCPA, so the 'outreach' leg of the plan is a compliance risk regardless of budget. If literally $0 is non-negotiable, Twilio is the wrong tool; treat it as a demo environment and budget ~$25 first month for any real deployment.

### Red flags

- Sandbox network egress blocked direct access to twilio.com, help.twilio.com, and web.archive.org — all 'primary' Twilio numbers here were extracted from official Twilio pages via search-engine-quoted content, not full page loads; re-verify the fee table in the Twilio Console before committing money.
- Per-SMS price conflict: 2026 sources report both $0.0079 and $0.0083 per US outbound segment; could not resolve against the live Twilio pricing page.
- 2026 Twilio pricing queries are dominated by SEO content farms (costbench.com, edesy.in, vbwebsol.com, etc.) with mutually inconsistent numbers (e.g., $0.013 vs $0.014/min voice); treated as low-weight corroboration only.
- The exact date Twilio switched trials from $15 credit to 'free units' was not verifiable; older tutorials still describe the $15 model — expect stale third-party guides.
- 2026 carrier-fee increases (AT&T Apr 1, Verizon May 1, T-Mobile Jan) come from secondary/aggregator sources only — medium confidence.
- The business plan's core assumption (AI-agent SMS outreach at $0) fails twice: economically (no free sending path exists) and legally (A2P campaign vetting requires opt-in consent; unsolicited outreach SMS risks TCPA liability of $500–$1,500 per message — the latter figure not re-verified this session).
- Trial anti-fraud: Twilio actively suspends accounts that look like they're circumventing trial limits or spinning up multiple trials; 'serial trial farming' is not a viable strategy and risks losing the founder's number and account.

### Summary

### Zero-budget implications

### Summary

The $0 outreach lane is far narrower than it looks. Every mainstream free ESP tier — Brevo (300/day), Mailjet (200/day, 6,000/mo), MailerSend (500/mo, cut from 3,000 in late 2025), Zoho Campaigns (6,000/mo) — explicitly prohibits cold/unsolicited email in its ToS or anti-spam policy, with opt-in consent required and account termination as the enforcement mechanism. Verified for all four. That leaves plain Gmail as the only free sending channel, and it is legal: CAN-SPAM is opt-out, not opt-in — cold email is lawful with truthful headers, ad identification, a physical postal address, and a working unsubscribe (penalties up to ~$53,088 per email for violations). But Gmail's 500-recipients/day cap is not a cold-email allowance. Deliverability sources converge on 20-30 cold emails/day per warmed inbox (ceiling ~50), ramping from 5-10/day for a new account, and uniformly advise against @gmail.com for cold outreach: no custom-domain SPF/DKIM/DMARC, amateur signal, and suspension risk under Gmail's own policies. Google's sender guidelines (Feb 2024, tightened through 2025-26) demand spam rates below 0.10% and never 0.30% for all senders — at 30 emails/day, a single spam complaint every ~10 days breaches that. LinkedIn automation is a ToS violation with heavy 2025-26 enforcement; not a free-substitute channel for an AI-agent stack. On conversion: Instantly's 2026 benchmark (billions of sends) shows a 3.43% average reply rate, declining yearly as AI slop floods inboxes; Belkins finds 5.1% average, rising to ~5.8-7% for sub-50-recipient personalized batches; 40-60% of replies are positive. Sources conflict on meetings: Belkins' aggregate is ~0.016 meetings/100 emails at scale, while optimized-campaign claims reach 2.34/100 — both are vendor-published. Realistic planning number for well-personalized, value-first (free-audit) small batches: 5-8% replies, 2-4 positive replies, and roughly 0.5-1.5 meetings per 100 sends.

### Zero-budget implications

Concretely: at $0 the founder's only ToS-compliant channel is one Gmail inbox sending ~20-30 highly personalized emails/day (start at 5-10/day for 4-6 weeks) — about 400-600 sends/month, not the 300/day ESP caps (those tiers ban cold email and will suspend the account) and not Gmail's 500/day ceiling (that volume from a fresh @gmail.com gets the account flagged). Expect roughly 20-50 total replies and 8-25 positive replies per month at benchmark rates for well-personalized value-first outreach (2-4 positive replies per 100 sends), yielding perhaps 2-8 conversations/meetings monthly — enough to close 1-3 small deals if the offer (e.g., free audit) is strong, but not a scalable AI-agent pipeline. Every scaling lever costs money: a custom domain (~$10/yr) plus Google Workspace or an outreach tool is the first mandatory spend, because deliverability experts unanimously say @gmail.com cold outreach caps trust and inbox placement. LinkedIn automation is not a free workaround — it is banned and actively enforced. AI sub-agents can do the research and drafting for free, but volume automation is exactly what the free tiers' ToS and Google's 0.10%/0.30% spam thresholds are built to catch. Plan for quality-over-quantity: 100 sends/week, deep personalization, physical address and unsubscribe line in every email, and a HubSpot free CRM (2 users, ~1,000 contacts for new accounts) to track it.

### Red flags

- All four checked ESP free tiers (Brevo, Mailjet, MailerSend, Zoho) prohibit cold/unsolicited email — using them for this plan risks account termination, so the '$0 outreach stack' has no legitimate ESP.
- Free tiers are shrinking: MailerSend cut its free plan from 3,000 to 500 emails/month (Oct-Dec 2025) and HubSpot cut free contact storage to ~1,000 for post-Sept-2024 accounts — $0 capacity quoted in older guides is stale.

- Google's 0.30% spam-rate threshold applies to all senders: at 30 sends/day, roughly one complaint per ~330 emails breaches it — tiny volumes have zero margin for error.

### Summary

The GEO market in Aug 2026 has three distinct price floors. (1) SaaS monitoring: entry points are $29/mo (Otterly Lite), $89/mo (SE Ranking AI add-on), $95/mo (Peec Starter), $99/mo (Profound Starter, public since June 2026; Semrush AI Toolkit per domain), rising to $245–$699/mo for serious prompt volume and $828+/mo for full Ahrefs Brand Radar. Local Falcon bundles AI-visibility checks into credit packs from $24.99/mo. Repricing is rapid — Profound's page showed no prices as recently as June 10, 2026. (2) Agencies: one-time GEO/AI-visibility audits sell from $99 productized to $500–$2,000 typical, $5,000 flat (Avante), and $8,000–$25,000 enterprise-grade. Retainers run $500–$1,500/mo for monitoring-only, $2,000–$8,000/mo mid-market, and $8,000–$20,000/mo at category leader First Page Sage; almost no agency publishes prices, so most figures come from competitor-written pricing guides. (3) Marketplaces: Fiverr GEO/AI-audit gigs cluster at $10–$100 (tiers to $395); the only sellers with large review counts (100–900+) are legacy SEO sellers who rebadged gigs, so verified purchase volume for pure GEO gigs is thin — a genuine skeptic flag. Upwork shows GEO job posts and $30–$150/hr AI-specialist rates but no GEO-specific benchmark. Demand evidence is real but two-sided: BrightLocal (n=1,002) finds 45% of US consumers used AI for local business recommendations (vs 6% in 2025), ChatGPT ecommerce referrals grew 210% YoY to 20.5M visits in Q1 2026, and 82–94% of surveyed marketers are allocating GEO budget — yet AI referrals are still only ~0.32% of total website traffic, so ROI-driven churn is a live risk. Caveat: the network proxy blocked direct access to all vendor pricing pages, so every figure here is a search-mediated snapshot of the cited page, not a page I opened; verify the handful you build a business on.

### Zero-budget implications

Concretely for a $0 founder: you cannot resell SaaS dashboards — the cheapest useful monitoring is $29–$99/mo and Semrush has no free trial, so free-tier arbitrage on tools is mostly closed (exceptions: Peec's 7-day trial and free AI-visibility checkers usable for one-off lead magnets). What is open is labor arbitrage on audits: an audit that agencies sell for $500–$5,000 can be produced with free ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini accounts plus AI sub-agents running manual prompt checks, at near-zero marginal cost. The realistic $0 play is (a) a productized GEO audit at $99–$500 — above Fiverr's $10–$100 race-to-the-bottom, 5–10x below agency floors — sold via free-audit-teaser outreach, then (b) converting buyers to $500–$1,500/mo monitoring-plus-fixes retainers, the lowest verified recurring tier real agencies charge. Do not build the plan on Fiverr demand: GEO-specific gigs show few verified purchases, and Fiverr takes 20% of the $20–$100 price points that dominate there. The BrightLocal 45% stat and the 82–94% budget-allocation surveys are your sales copy; the 0.32%-of-traffic reality is your churn risk — price for 3-month payback, not annual contracts you can't enforce.

### Red flags

- All vendor pricing pages were unreachable directly (egress proxy); every SaaS figure is a search-index snapshot of the cited page, not a page actually opened — verify before quoting to a client.
- Fiverr proof-of-purchase is weak: pure GEO/AI-visibility gigs cluster at $10–$100 with few visible reviews; the 100–900+ review counts belong to legacy SEO sellers who added GEO keywords, so their review history is not evidence of GEO buying.
- BrightLocal's 6%→45% one-year jump in consumers using AI for local recommendations is suspiciously large — plausibly partly a question-framing change between survey waves; treat 45% as an upper bound.
- AI referral traffic is still ~0.32% of total website traffic despite 16x growth — buyers paying $99–$699/mo for monitoring may churn when referral ROI stays invisible.
- Nearly all agency pricing figures come from content-marketing 'pricing guides' written by competing agencies/tools, not surveys with disclosed methodology; only First Page Sage's tiers were independently torn down.
- Sources conflict on specifics: Scrunch $250 vs $300 entry; Profound $99/$399 public tiers vs a $499 'Lite' in earlier snapshots; ChatGPT's AI-referral share 63% vs 75% vs 92% across three studies.
- Pricing is repricing fast (Profound published prices only on 2026-06-11; Semrush restructured into 'Semrush One' tiers) — any numbers older than ~90 days in this niche should be rechecked.
- Marketing-budget-shift stats (94% increasing, 12–24% of budgets) come from vendor-adjacent surveys with undisclosed samples — use as directional, not as sizing.
- Session web-search budget was exhausted before I could pull per-gig Fiverr review counts and named mid-tier agency rate cards; those two gaps remain unverified (low confidence).

### Summary

The local SEO market in Aug 2026 shows a wide, bimodal price structure. Survey benchmarks: the most common SEO retainer bracket is $501–$1,000/month (Ahrefs, 20.4% of respondents; local-market SEOs averaged $1,557/mo — but that survey is ~2018 vintage), Backlinko's 300+-respondent survey (updated Dec 2025) puts typical retainers at $1,000–$2,500/mo, and BrightLocal's Local Search Industry Survey found a median minimum retainer of $500–$699 (2019) with solo consultants averaging $766/mo per client (2020). No genuinely current (2025–2026) survey median exists. Premium ceiling: Sterling Sky charges $2,100+ for a one-off local SEO audit and $3,000–$5,000/mo retainers; BrightLocal's own managed service runs $799–$1,299/location/mo. One-off audits generally sell for $300–$2,000 (freelancer) to $1,000–$5,000 (agency). GBP optimization one-offs: US micro-agencies productize at $250–$499 (ProfileBoost $399, Review Overhaul $400, Blessed Arc $250), while Fiverr sellers deliver the same SKU for $25–$50 (one seller: $50, 5.0 stars, 550+ reviews; a suspension-reinstatement specialist: $100, 2,600+ reviews). Citations: ceiling is BrightLocal at $3.20/site and $30/aggregator, Whitespark at ~$4–5/listing ($400 top-20 package, $2/listing updates); floor is Fiverr at $5 for 250–350 citations — a fully commoditized race to the bottom. Review management: platforms charge $299–$699/mo/location (Birdeye) and ~$399–$599/mo quote-only (Podium, real cost $450–$800), versus $19–$49/mo budget tools, leaving a wide gap where boutique human services charge $500–$1,500/mo. Saturation signals are strong at the floor (830k+ Fiverr sellers, SEO called a 'red ocean', AI-generated pricing content flooding search), weak at the trust-based top.

### Zero-budget implications

Concretely for a $0-spend founder: (1) Don't sell citations — the Fiverr floor is $5 for 300, and an AI pipeline can't undercut that; only bundle citations inside a package priced against BrightLocal ($3.20/site) as the reference ceiling. (2) The viable one-off SKU is GBP optimization/audit at $150–$400: real US sellers clear $250–$400 productized, and the Fiverr floor ($25–$50) applies only to anonymous new accounts — but a new account with zero reviews starts at the floor, so expect $25–$75 for the first 10–20 sales while building review count (the 550-review $50 seller shows the grind). (3) The realistic recurring offer is GBP management + review response at $99–$299/mo, positioned against Merchynt ($99–$400/mo) and far under Birdeye/Podium ($299–$699/mo); at the BrightLocal-surveyed solo average of ~$766/mo per client, 5–10 clients is a $4–8k/mo business, and even at a cautious $200/mo it takes only 10 clients to reach $2k/mo. (4) The binding constraint isn't production (AI handles audits, posts, review drafts on free tiers) — it's distribution and trust: marketplace listing is free but saturated; cold outreach at volume usually requires paid email/data tools, so the free path is manual prospecting from Google Maps data + a handful of personalized audits per day as lead magnets. (5) Compliance guardrail: automated review solicitation/response must respect the FTC fake-review rule and Google's policies, or client GBP suspensions destroy the business — note there's a proven $100/fix market in un-suspending profiles, which is itself a candidate SKU that AI can research but a human must execute carefully.

### Red flags

- Access limitation: the research sandbox's egress proxy blocked direct fetches of every commercial domain (ahrefs.com, backlinko.com, sterlingsky.ca, brightlocal.com, whitespark.ca, fiverr.com, birdeye.com), and the shared WebSearch budget ran out mid-task. All numbers come from search-indexed snapshots of the named primary URLs, not pages I opened — single-sourced figures deserve independent re-verification before pricing decisions.
- The most-cited 'survey medians' are stale: Ahrefs' pricing study ($1,557/mo local average, $501–$1,000 most common bracket) was fielded around 2018–2019, and BrightLocal's Local Search Industry Survey pricing questions date to 2019–2020 ($500–$699 median minimum, $766 solo average). No 2025–2026 industry-wide survey with published medians surfaced; the Whitespark 'Local Search Industry Survey' the task named appears to actually be BrightLocal's series (Whitespark runs the Local Search Ranking Factors report, which has no pricing data).
- Sources conflict on Sterling Sky's retainer floor ($3,000/mo vs $4,000/mo on their own pages) and on Birdeye Starter ($299 vs $349/mo depending on billing term) — both reported.
- Much of the 2026 'how much does X cost' content is near-identical AI-generated aggregator material (webtonic.io, checkthat.ai, replifast.com, etc.) that recycles the same old survey numbers, so apparent convergence across sources is partly an echo chamber, not independent confirmation.
- Severe commoditization at the floor: $5 Fiverr gigs claim 250–350 citations and $25–$50 GBP optimizations with hundreds of 5-star reviews — exactly the deliverables an AI-agent pipeline would produce. Practitioner forums (Local Search Forum) warn Fiverr citations are low quality, but buyers who shop on price can't tell the difference, which caps what an anonymous new seller can charge.
- Compliance risk for the review-management stream: the FTC's rule on fake/incentivized reviews (16 CFR Part 465, effective Oct 2024 — from my knowledge base; could not re-verify this session, low confidence) makes automated review solicitation/response services legally sensitive; AI-generated review responses and review gating can also violate Google's policies and get client profiles suspended.
- Demand-side uncertainty (unverified): 2025–2026 rollout of AI Overviews/AI Mode in Google is widely claimed to be shifting local discovery; I could not verify its measured effect on local SEO demand before the search budget ran out.
- The '68% of small businesses outsource SEO' figure comes from an unsourced listicle — treat as marketing folklore, not data.

### Summary

Demand-side evidence for AI-visibility services is real but lopsided. Verified live today on GitHub: the GEO builder ecosystem is in a hockey stick — 486 repos match "generative engine optimization," of which only 2 predate 2025, 76 were created in 2025, and 408 in Jan–Aug 2026; curated lists (fetched today) catalog 70+ commercial tools priced $29–$499/mo, 24+ dedicated platforms, 10+ specialized agencies, and VC funding (Profound reportedly $35M Series B). Adoption-side numbers (mostly 2025, training memory or secondary citations — not re-verifiable from this sandbox): ChatGPT at 700–800M WAU (Aug–Oct 2025 official), ~5.2B visits/month and ~86–87% of all AI referral traffic; AI/LLM referrals to business sites growing +357% to +800% YoY (2025), Adobe measured +1,200% to retail (Feb 2025), and AI-referred visitors convert 2x–4.4x better (Conductor/Semrush, conflicting multipliers). Net: rising real demand signal, concentrated in tech/B2B, with fast commoditization and an effectiveness question mark at its core.

### Zero-budget implications

Concretely for a $0 founder: (1) Tooling cost is genuinely zero — MIT-licensed self-hostable GEO/AEO auditors with real traction exist today (onvoyage-ai/gtm-engineer-skills 1,287 stars, Auriti-Labs/geo-optimizer-skill 739 stars, elmohq/elmo 266 stars), so AI sub-agents can produce audit deliverables without paid SaaS. (2) But the free 'AI visibility report' is already the industry-standard lead magnet (AthenaHQ, Trakkr, Quno give it away), so the report itself has near-zero market price; the monetizable step is implementation (schema, entity consistency, fresh FAQ/pricing pages, Bing IndexNow) — which official engine docs actually endorse, unlike GEO tricks. (3) Anchor pricing exists at $29–$499/mo for tools; a solo productized service realistically slots into the $100–$500/mo SMB band, but with zero verified evidence SMBs are asking for this, every sale is an education-heavy cold sale — bad fit for fully-automated outreach on free email/social tiers with tight sending limits. (4) Do not promise AI rankings: NeurIPS-grade evidence says content tactics mostly don't work and citation behavior is 95% unexplained — promise auditable hygiene fixes and before/after mention tracking instead, or refund risk and reputation damage follow. (5) Speed matters: 408 new GEO repos in 8 months means the window for a generic offer is closing; a defensible $0 play is a tight niche (one vertical, one metro) where the founder's sub-agents track actual ChatGPT/Gemini answers about local providers and sell the delta, while treating GEO as an upsell on ordinary local-SEO deliverables that demand data actually supports.
